Workplace Safety Rules and Regulations

Workers must follow strict safety protocols to prevent accidents and injuries on the job site. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) is mandatory in all hazardous areas. This includes hard hats, safety goggles, gloves, high-visibility vests, and steel-toed boots.

All employees must complete a safety induction before starting work on any new site. This covers emergency procedures, first aid locations, fire exit routes, and the reporting process for hazards or near-misses.

Electrical safety is paramount. Workers must never tamper with electrical panels without proper certification. Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) procedures must be followed whenever electrical equipment is being maintained or repaired to prevent accidental energization.

Chemical handling requires special care. Safety Data Sheets (SDS) must be read and understood before handling any hazardous substances. Proper storage, labeling, and disposal of chemicals are mandatory to prevent contamination and health hazards.

Working at heights poses serious risks. Scaffolding must be inspected before use, and harnesses must be worn when working above 1.8 meters. No worker should work at height alone; a buddy system is required.

Fire safety is everyone's responsibility. Flammable materials must be stored away from ignition sources. Fire extinguishers must be accessible and workers must know how to use them. Regular fire drills are conducted every quarter.

Slips, trips, and falls are the most common workplace accidents. Keeping walkways clear, cleaning spills immediately, and ensuring adequate lighting in all areas significantly reduces these incidents.
